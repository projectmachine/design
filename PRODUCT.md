# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Mixed teams: designers, engineers, and PMs collaborating on design artifacts together. Users arrive from different backgrounds and different levels of design confidence. A senior designer uses it to run critique and iterate fast; an engineer uses it to extract specs and tokens; a PM uses it to understand what's shipping. Context of use: focused work sessions, often switching between reviewing, generating, and handing off. The common thread is that no one is a passive viewer — everyone is a participant.

## Product Purpose

Open Design is a design platform where AI agents are woven into the workflow rather than bolted on. It covers the full design loop: generating design from prompts, reviewing and annotating existing work, producing code-ready specs and tokens, and managing design systems over time. Success looks like a mixed team being able to move from idea to shipped UI without losing fidelity at the handoff points, and without needing a dedicated design specialist to unlock each step.

## Brand Personality

Warm, collaborative, human. Approachable without being soft — the product should feel like a skilled colleague rather than a tool or a chatbot. Design has historically been a discipline with high gatekeeping; Open Design's personality should consistently lower that barrier without sacrificing capability. Voice is direct, specific, and never jargony.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS cream** (Notion clones, Linear-alike dashboards): the off-white background, muted purple/blue accent, rounded card grids, and flat typographic hierarchy that defines interchangeable B2B SaaS. Open Design should be identifiably warmer and more considered.
- **AI-hype aesthetics** (Midjourney, Runway, AI-adjacent tools): glowing purple gradients, neon on near-black, geometric particle effects, or anything that signals "this was made by AI" as a flex. Capability should speak through the output, not the chrome.

## Design Principles

1. **Human over machine** — AI is a partner in the workflow, not the hero of the interface. Warmth and human legibility come first; capability is assumed, not performed.
2. **Accessible from day one** — Design tools have historically excluded non-designers. Every interaction should lower the barrier to participation: clear labels, predictable structure, no expertise tax.
3. **Collaboration as foreground** — Mixed teams share context constantly. Shared work, annotations, and handoffs should feel native to the surface, not secondary.
4. **Confidence through calm** — Fast, capable tools don't need to announce themselves. The interface should project expertise through restraint: deliberate choices, not decoration.
5. **Open, not generic** — "Open" is load-bearing. The experience should feel expansive and generous: more surface area, not less; more disclosure, not more gating.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Keyboard navigation throughout. Sufficient color contrast for all text and interactive states. Screen reader support for primary workflows. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for all transitions and animations.
